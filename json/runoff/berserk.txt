- As the story develops, even as much as it pretends it’s not sometimes, the unbearable rage towards griffith and the unbearable pain toward what has happened to caska fade. Not that he stops caring about them, but they become fuelled only by the memory of them existing - he still definitely hates griffith, but he hasn’t seen the guy in like 2 years and his “neverending burning hate” is really just, without him knowing, him forcing himself to keep the hate alive by burning parts of himself to keep it alive since no active kindling is being thrown on it (griffith has done nothing else)
- Similarly, his pain towards caska fades - despite how potent is anguish is at what has happened, he has had a “new caska” for like 2 years or some shit. He is not desperate to get the current caska back, he is desperate to get the original caska back, which is not the current caska
- So, a mental imbalance starts to form from the fact that the hate and pain he is carying around all lives years in the past, and not only has it been mitigated by the new adventures he’s had with the new party and their newfound importance to him (schierke, farnese, puck, etc) but it’s also just difficult to hold onto hate and pain that long without it naturally fading away - hence, he is having to burn parts of himself just to keep the hate and pain and desire for revenge alive - or, in the symbolic sense of what it would mean for the series, he’s burning himself up outside in order to keep burning himself up inside
- It’s a self destructive obsession that can’t be fulfilled because it categorically lives in the past rather than the present. He doesn’t have to get revenge, he has to grieve
- Which is WHY, I think, caska should die. Perhaps when Farnese and Schierke attempt to get her memory back - something happens and goes wrong and instead of getting her memories back they literally kill her, or permenantly destroy her memories by accicdent so there is no hope of getting her back at all, etc
- Why? Am I just mad at caska and want her to die?
- NO, it’s because doing that would force gatts to face the situation where he has to grieve her death, but he realizes it feels wrong - he realizes he’s not grieving her death, he’s grieving the loss of his drive for revenge, he’s grieving the loss of her 2 years ago. Afterall, he had not (in any meaningful capacity) been attached to her current form - if anything, he resented it for what it represented and what had been stolen from him. In essence, current caska dying would force gatts to realize that caska did not actually just die, but that she died 2 years ago. Or rather, that he had lost her 2 years ago, not in the current time, and he had been holding on to a memory trying to transplant it into the current day - a space where the memory has no place being anymore, ESPECIALLY since that “companion void” has been “filled” by schierke, by farnese, by puck, etc. Other people have grown more important to him, and he is forced to reckon with the fact that he hasn’t been able to allow himself to realize how important they all are to him because he was so blinded by this hope to get this person back that he had already lost but just hadn’t realized it yet
- In context of the real world; sometimes you fucking lose people. Sometimes the people are incredibly important to you, but sometimes they just leave your life - not even necessarily by death, sometimes an event happens or times change or whatever and there comes a point, a sudden point, where you never see them again, although you think about them for the rest of your life. Being able to handle that is a really sobering and maturing moment for people. It’s not that they mean less to you once they’re gone - it’s just that they’re gone.
- And that’s exactly what gatts so badly needs to do to complete his arc imo - he has to see caska die so he can feel the full heartbreak, and then realize the thing causing the heartbreak happened 2 years ago rather than just now, and from there realize, you know, the importance of the friends that are still currently in his life and everything else he has built up in the interim without his consciously realizing it

Revenge, un general unwillingness to move on, will not make you feel whole; it is fundamentally only a means to distract yourself from the grieving and growing you need to do, and can affect those you love if you don’t control yourself.
